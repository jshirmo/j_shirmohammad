Work / Research

Neural Mechanisms of Age-Dependent Seizures

Tasker Lab · Tulane University

As an undergraduate researcher, Jasmine contributes to the behavioral and tissue-based portions of an ongoing project investigating age-dependent spontaneous seizures in alpha-1A adrenergic receptor (α1A-AR) knockout mice. These mice develop spontaneous seizures beginning at approximately six months of age, but the brain regions, neuronal subtypes, and mechanisms involved have not yet been fully identified.

Research question

Which neural populations contribute?

Which brain regions and neuronal subtypes contribute to the development of spontaneous seizures in α1A-adrenergic receptor knockout mice? The project examines how loss of this receptor affects behavior and seizure susceptibility.

Behavioral contribution

Observe behavior and seizure onset

Jasmine monitors animals for the onset and severity of spontaneous seizures, helping determine when seizure activity begins as they age. Her work also includes animal handling and maintenance, open-field testing, stress/anxiety-related behavioral testing, experimental data collection, and data analysis.

Tissue-based contribution

Examine the brain alongside behavior

Jasmine performs brain cryosectioning and helps prepare and examine brain sections under the microscope. Through brain-section staining and imaging where appropriate, she contributes tissue-based observations that help investigate the brain regions and neuronal populations associated with the observed seizure phenotype.

Across levels of neuroscience

Connect behavior to brain tissue

One of the most valuable parts of this project is working across different levels of neuroscience: observing behavioral phenotypes in living animals, then examining neural tissue through cryosectioning, staining, imaging, and microscopy. This makes it possible to consider behavioral findings alongside cellular and anatomical findings without assuming that a causal region or subtype has already been identified.

Study context

Behavior, stress, and seizure susceptibility

The broader study uses behavioral and seizure-related assays such as open-field testing, anxiety/stress testing, fear-related behavioral testing, seizure monitoring, and seizure-susceptibility assays. Jasmine’s individual responsibilities are the behavioral and tissue-based activities described above.

Why this research matters

Understanding adrenergic signaling and seizure risk

Adrenergic receptors help regulate neural activity throughout the brain. Investigating how loss of the α1A receptor contributes to spontaneous seizures may improve understanding of the relationship among adrenergic signaling, neuronal excitability, stress-related circuitry, and seizure susceptibility. Identifying the causal brain region or neuronal subtype remains an ongoing research question.
