Work / Research

Neural Mechanisms of Age-Dependent Seizures

Tasker Lab · Tulane University

As an undergraduate researcher, Jasmine contributes to an ongoing project investigating age-dependent spontaneous seizures in alpha-1A adrenergic receptor (α1A-AR) knockout mice. Her work connects behavioral observation with brain-tissue analysis to help investigate the neural populations involved.

Visit the Tasker Lab (opens in a new tab)

Research question

Which neural populations contribute?

Which brain regions and neuronal subtypes contribute to spontaneous seizures in α1A-AR knockout mice?

Model

Age-dependent seizure onset

These mice develop spontaneous seizures beginning at approximately six months of age.

Goal

Connect receptor loss to phenotype

Examine how loss of α1A-AR affects behavior and seizure susceptibility.

My contribution

Behavioral and tissue-based research

I contribute to both sides of the project: observing seizure-related behavior in living animals and examining neural tissue to investigate the anatomical and cellular context of that phenotype.

Behavioral neuroscience

Observe behavior and seizure onset

  • Monitor animals for the onset and severity of spontaneous seizures
  • Help determine when seizure activity begins as animals age
  • Conduct open-field and stress/anxiety-related behavioral testing
  • Handle and maintain animals

The broader study also includes fear-related behavioral and seizure-susceptibility assays; my responsibilities are listed above.

Histology, imaging & analysis

Examine neural tissue

  • Perform brain cryosectioning
  • Prepare and examine brain sections using microscopy
  • Support brain-section staining and imaging where appropriate
  • Collect and analyze experimental data

Connecting the pieces

From behavior to brain tissue

Working across these levels of neuroscience allows behavioral findings to be considered alongside cellular and anatomical observations when investigating the brain regions and neuronal populations associated with seizure behavior. Identifying a causal region or subtype remains an ongoing research question.

Why this research matters

Adrenergic signaling and seizure susceptibility

Adrenergic receptors help regulate neural activity throughout the brain. Investigating how loss of α1A-AR contributes to spontaneous seizures may improve understanding of the relationship among adrenergic signaling, neuronal excitability, stress-related circuitry, and seizure susceptibility.
